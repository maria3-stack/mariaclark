The Discovery Problem

Most authors don't have a quality problem. They have a visibility problem. Your book can be exceptional and still be invisible to the algorithm that's supposed to introduce it to the world.

← Back to Home

Why Good Books Stay Invisible

Every day, thousands of well-written, professionally edited, beautifully covered books launch straight into silence. Their authors did everything "right" — and months later, they're still asking why no one's buying.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: readers aren't rejecting these books. They're never seeing them.

Amazon alone holds over 15 million titles. In that ocean, your book is one drop — invisible by default, unless you build the infrastructure that puts it in front of the right reader at the right moment.

A decade of studying what separates visible books from invisible ones taught me this: visibility isn't about shouting louder. It's about standing exactly where your ideal reader is already looking.

The gap between a book that sells 50 copies and one that sells 5,000 is rarely writing quality. It's almost always positioning.

What Is a Discovery Gap?

A discovery gap is the distance between where your book sits today and where your ideal reader is actually looking. It's rarely one clean break. It's usually several small misalignments stacking up:

  • Category drift — filed under "Contemporary Fiction" while readers browse "Small Town Romance."
  • Keyword blindness — metadata optimized for terms nobody types.
  • Also-bought decay — recommendation carousel drifted toward unrelated books.
  • Pricing mismatch — price signals the wrong genre tier.
  • Blurb confusion — description doesn't trip the genre promise readers scan for.
  • Platform absence — not where your readers gather.
  • Community disconnect — book clubs and influencers don't know it exists.

Every one is measurable. Every one is fixable. Close them systematically and readers start finding the book on their own.

"I spent two years trying everything. It wasn't until Maria Jane showed me my book was in the wrong categories, targeting keywords nobody searches for, that anything changed."

— Romance Author, after repositioning

8 Signs Your Book Has a Discovery Problem

Recognize two or three? Your book almost certainly has a fixable positioning gap.

📉

"My reviews are great but nobody's finding the book"

Quality and visibility are separate problems. Amazon doesn't promote a book just because it's well-rated.

🚀

"I launched and it just... disappeared"

Launch momentum is a narrow window. If categories and keywords aren't locked in, the algorithm moves on.

🧭

"I don't know who my readers are"

You can't be positioned for a reader you can't describe. Audience mapping surfaces communities you didn't know existed.

🗂️

"I think I'm in the wrong categories"

A common, correct instinct. Broad categories bury you; micro-categories can put you on a bestseller list.

📚

"Readers love Book 1 but never buy Book 2"

That's a funnel leak, not a writing problem. Back matter, blurbs, and series metadata usually need work.

📦

"I have a backlist that's just sitting there"

Older titles decay quietly as categories and keywords go stale. A metadata refresh can give a years-old book a second life.

😵‍💫

"Marketing feels completely overwhelming"

Usually a prioritization problem. You need to be told what to ignore as much as what to do.

💸

"I'm spending money but can't tell if it's working"

Ads pointed at the wrong category burn budget without reaching a reader who'd buy. Fix positioning first.

Why Traditional Marketing Often Fails

Most book marketing advice is about promotion — get the word out, build buzz, make noise. But promotion without positioning is turning up the volume on static.

"Before Maria Jane, I'd tried everything. She showed me my positioning was broken: I was marketing to the wrong people in the wrong places."

— Thriller Author

The Foundation Has to Come First

  • Correct category placement — specific subcategories your readers actually shop.
  • Aligned keywords — matched to what readers actually type.
  • A blurb that tells the truth fast — readers know within seconds if it's "for them."
  • Pricing that signals the right tier.
  • Presence where readers gather — varies enormously by genre.
  • Clear sense of your comp titles — where you sit next to books readers already love.

The Bottom Line

Every discovery problem here is fixable. None of it is about talent, luck, or connections — it's infrastructure and positioning, and both can be analyzed, measured, and repaired methodically.

My Story

I didn't set out to become a book marketing specialist. Like many people in this industry, I stumbled into it by noticing patterns others seemed to miss.

It started with a question I couldn't stop thinking about: why do some books with excellent reviews stay completely invisible, while others with seemingly weaker content find their audience?

I analyzed book after book — BSR patterns, category placements, keyword strategies, review velocities, also-bought carousels. And gradually, a pattern emerged.

The difference was positioning. Visible books were positioned where their natural readers could find them. Invisible books were structurally impossible for their ideal readers to discover.
